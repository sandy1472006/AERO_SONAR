## 1. Introduction

A key technical failure mode in amateur sonar transmitters is "black-box guesswork"—arbitrarily mapping sensor readings to transmission parameters through arbitrary heuristic functions without underlying physical justification.

**AERO-SONAR** is built entirely on validated, empirical ocean acoustic physics models. Every decision generated by the Environment-Energy-Waveform (EWA) engine is directly traceable to peer-reviewed acoustic derivations, calibrated using real **Argo Float Ocean Observations (INCOIS)** and the operating envelope of India's indigenous **Maya AUV (CSIR-NIO Goa)**.

## 2. Speed of Sound in Seawater — Mackenzie (1981)

Sound velocity in seawater is a non-linear function of temperature ($T$, °C), salinity ($S$, ppt or PSU), and hydrostatic depth ($D$, m). AERO-SONAR evaluates the Mackenzie 9-term empirical formulation:

$$\begin{aligned}
c(T, S, D) = 1448.96 &+ 4.591\,T - 5.304 \times 10^{-2}\,T^2 + 2.374 \times 10^{-4}\,T^3 \\
&+ 1.340\,(S - 35) + 1.630 \times 10^{-2}\,D + 1.675 \times 10^{-7}\,D^2 \\
&- 1.025 \times 10^{-2}\,T\,(S - 35) - 7.139 \times 10^{-13}\,T\,D^3
\end{aligned}$$

- **Operational Range:** $T \in [0, 30]\,^\circ\text{C}$, $S \in [25, 40]\,\text{ppt}$, $D \in [0, 200]\,\text{m}$.
- **Accuracy:** Standard error of $\pm 0.070\,\text{m/s}$ relative to direct CTD pulse-echo measurements.
- **Role in Transmitter:** Accurate sound speed calculation is mandatory for setting pulse duration ($\tau$), predicting two-way travel time, and tuning the matched-filter Doppler compression factor.

## 3. Multicomponent Seawater Acoustic Absorption — François-Garrison (1982)

Acoustic energy propagating through the water column attenuates through geometric spreading and intrinsic absorption. Total seawater absorption $\alpha_{\text{sw}}(f)$ in $\text{dB/km}$ arises from three distinct physical relaxation mechanisms:

$$\alpha_{\text{sw}}(f) = \frac{A_1 P_1 f_1 f^2}{f_1^2 + f^2} + \frac{A_2 P_2 f_2 f^2}{f_2^2 + f^2} + A_3 P_3 f^2 \quad [\text{dB/km}]$$

### 3.1 Component 1: Boric Acid Chemical Relaxation ($B(OH)_3$)
Dominates below 10 kHz; accounts for chemical dissociation relaxation:
$$A_1 = \frac{8.86}{c} \cdot 10^{(0.78\,\text{pH} - 5)}$$
$$f_1 = 2.8 \left(\frac{S}{35}\right)^{0.5} 10^{(4 - \frac{1245}{T + 273})}$$
$$P_1 = 1.0$$

### 3.2 Component 2: Magnesium Sulfate Relaxation ($MgSO_4$)
**The dominant absorption mechanism across AERO-SONAR's 70 kHz – 500 kHz frequency band:**
$$A_2 = 21.44 \left(1 + \frac{0.025\,T}{c}\right) \left(\frac{S}{35}\right)$$
$$f_2 = \frac{8.17 \cdot 10^{(8.7 - \frac{1990}{T + 273})}}{1 + 0.0018\,(S - 35)}$$
$$P_2 = 1 - 1.37 \times 10^{-4}\,D + 6.2 \times 10^{-9}\,D^2$$

### 3.3 Component 3: Pure Water Viscous Attenuation
Arises from shear and volume bulk viscosity:
$$A_3 = 3.96 \times 10^{-4} \cdot 10^{-(1.52 - 0.046\,T + 0.00067\,T^2)}$$
$$P_3 = 1 - 3.83 \times 10^{-5}\,D + 4.9 \times 10^{-10}\,D^2$$

### Why Frequency Drops in Deep or Estuarine Waters:
At 100 kHz, absorption in seawater is $\approx 34.2\text{ dB/km}$. At 500 kHz, absorption skyrockets to **$143.0\text{ dB/km}$**! Over a two-way round trip of 150 m ($0.3\text{ km}$ path), 500 kHz suffers $42.9\text{ dB}$ of pure absorption loss, whereas 100 kHz suffers only $10.2\text{ dB}$—a massive **$32.7\text{ dB}$ link budget advantage** for low frequency.

## 4. Acoustic Scattering by Suspended Sediment — Thorne & Hanes (2002)

In turbid waters (estuaries, dredged harbors, and river discharge plumes), suspended particulates scatter acoustic energy out of the beam. Thorne & Hanes formulated the particulate scattering coefficient $\alpha_{\text{turb}}$ as a function of turbidity ($NTU$) and grain radius ($a_s$):

$$\alpha_{\text{turb}}(f) = \frac{k_s \cdot M}{\rho_s \cdot a_s} \cdot \left(\frac{f}{f_{\text{ref}}}\right)^\gamma \quad [\text{dB/km}]$$

In AERO-SONAR's calibrated real-time estimator:
$$\alpha_{\text{turb}}(f, \text{NTU}) = 0.180 \cdot \text{NTU} \cdot \left(\frac{f_{\text{kHz}}}{100}\right)^{1.25} \quad [\text{dB/km}]$$

**Total Operating Absorption:**
$$\alpha_{\text{total}} = \alpha_{\text{sw}}(f, T, S, D) + \alpha_{\text{turb}}(f, \text{NTU})$$

## 5. Active Sonar Link Budget — Urick (1983)

The transmitter power decision $A$ (amplitude %) is governed by satisfying the Active Sonar Equation:

$$\text{SNR} = \text{SL} - 2\text{TL} + \text{TS} - (\text{NL} - \text{DI}) + G_p \ge \text{DT} + \text{Margin}$$

- **Source Level ($\text{SL}$):** $\text{SL} = \text{SL}_{\text{max}} + 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{A}{100}\right)$ (dB re $1\,\mu\text{Pa}$ @ $1\,\text{m}$)
- **Two-Way Transmission Loss ($2\text{TL}$):**
  $$2\text{TL} = 40 \log_{10}(R) + 2 \cdot \left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{total}}}{1000}\right) \cdot R \quad [\text{dB}]$$
- **Target Strength ($\text{TS}$):** Typical seabed / obstacle cross-section: $-14\,\text{dB}$ to $-20\,\text{dB}$.
- **Noise Level ($\text{NL}$):** Ambient acoustic noise floor in dB, modeled via Wenz (1962).
- **Directivity Index ($\text{DI}$):** Piezo projector beam directivity ($+14\,\text{dB}$).
- **Detection Threshold ($\text{DT}$):** Receiver matched-filter threshold ($+8\,\text{dB}$).
- **Pulse Compression Processing Gain ($G_p$):**
  $$G_p = 10 \log_{10}(B \cdot \tau) \quad [\text{dB}]$$

## 6. Doppler Invariance & Waveform Switching Criterion

When an AUV travels at forward velocity $v$ relative to target or water particles, the received echo experiences a Doppler frequency shift factor $\eta = \frac{c - v}{c + v} \approx 1 - \frac{2v}{c}$.

For an LFM chirp, Doppler causes an apparent time shift (range-Doppler ambiguity). If Doppler exceeds a critical threshold, matched-filter correlator correlation degrades severely. The dimensionless **Doppler Index $D$** is defined as:

$$D = \frac{2 \cdot v \cdot B \cdot \tau}{c}$$

| Doppler Index ($D$) | Acoustic Effect | AERO-SONAR Autonomous Action |
|---|---|---|
| **$D \le 0.8$** | Negligible Doppler smearing; correlation peak loss $<0.5\text{ dB}$ | Standard LFM Chirp maintained with zero correction overhead. |
| **$0.8 < D \le 2.0$** | Moderate correlation degradation (SNR penalty $1 - 3\text{ dB}$) | LFM bandwidth slightly adjusted; SNR margin increased. |
| **$D > 2.0$** | Severe Doppler mismatch; LFM correlation peak bifurcates | **Automatic switch to Hyperbolic Frequency Modulated (HFM) pulse.** HFM is geometrically scale-invariant, eliminating Doppler mismatch completely. |

## 7. Range Resolution & Operating Trade-offs

The theoretical slant-range resolution of a matched-filter sonar receiver is inversely proportional to swept acoustic bandwidth $B$:

$$\Delta R = \frac{c}{2 B}$$

- At $500\text{ kHz}$ carrier ($B = 75\text{ kHz}$):
  $$\Delta R = \frac{1500\,\text{m/s}}{2 \times 75000\,\text{Hz}} = 0.010\,\text{m} = \mathbf{1.0\,\text{cm}}$$
- At $100\text{ kHz}$ carrier ($B = 15\text{ kHz}$):
  $$\Delta R = \frac{1500\,\text{m/s}}{2 \times 15000\,\text{Hz}} = 0.050\,\text{m} = \mathbf{5.0\,\text{cm}}$$

This demonstrates the fundamental engineering trade-off: **500 kHz gives $5\times$ sharper imaging resolution (1 cm)**, but can only be sustained in shallow, clean water where $\alpha_{\text{total}}$ remains manageable.
